# Combat Statistics

Ordinary optional Worker plugin. Build with `pnpm --filter @ffcafe/mojito-combat-statistics build`; install/register `dist/plugin.json` using the existing plugin workflow. Required capabilities are session/game/events/data read, ordinary UI registration and `services.provide`. The plugin offers its overlay data as the `combat.statistics.overlay` service; the compatibility overlay templates are separate plugins in `@ffcafe/mojito-overlay-plugins` that consume it. No filesystem or network permission is requested.

Direct normalized effects, target HP (`hpChanged`) and host periodic results share one ordered subscription. Unsupported periodic definitions fall back to direct statistics and an explicit partial-data message. Actor identity uses source generations and observed entity lifetimes. The parse scope setting selects whose damage is counted: `party` (default) covers the local player and own-party members, `alliance` covers everyone in the party list including other alliance parties, and `all` covers every player character and NPC party member. NPC party members of squadrons, Trusts and Duty Support (`BattleNpcSubKind` 9) are not in the party list of the game, so every scope counts them as members by their sub kind; fights in logs written before the sub kind was recorded are recomputed without them, and a replayed recording leaves out the NPC party members spawned before it started. Each scope includes the confirmed pets of its members. Settings saved before this option existed read as `party`. Saving a new scope reclassifies known actors immediately; damage already counted as out of scope is not restored. The `all` scope does not depend on party data, and other players are never inferred to be party members from job/name. NPC evidence and hostile effects are required to start a fight. Unknown owner, special effect directions and missing party/player data remain limitations rather than guessed attribution.

Auto mode closes after ten source seconds without hostile activity (configurable 3–120 seconds). Healing does not extend that deadline. Manual mode retains the encounter until explicitly ended or a source/zone boundary. Duration uses first/last accepted facts, not the quiet waiting period. Periodic revisions replace original allocations in their original encounter. The last ended encounter is **not** republished to legacy overlays after a post revision until template history behavior is verified; authoritative history is updated.

The ledger reads who gives and who takes an effect from its `rawFlags`, ignoring the extended-value bit 0x40. A heal flagged 0x80 lands on the caster of the action: it is the caster's own healing and healing taken, and its overheal is estimated against the caster's HP. Damage flagged 0xA0 is reflected by the target onto the enemy that hit it: it counts as damage of the reflecting player on the skill `reflect` (shown as 反伤), outside swings, hits, the maximum hit and the critical/direct-hit rates, and rDPS keeps all of it with that player because the host splits no buffs from it. Effect types that carry no damage or healing amount (combos, statuses, gauges, enmity; among them types 24, 25, 31, 52, 61, 62 and 73) are skipped, with or without flag 0x80. Any other flags or type counts as an unknown effect and marks the metrics of the fight as partial data.

Overheal is estimated from the last target HP received before the heal landed: a direct heal is paired with the HP of its EffectResult by action sequence, or, when no EffectResult arrives within five seconds (the game often sends none for a target at full HP), uses the target HP when its actionEffect arrived; a HoT tick uses the HP received before it. Healing that lands without a known target HP counts as effective healing and not as overheal, and lowers the quality of the overheal metrics. Damage is observed/estimated actor damage. rDPS (`contributionDps`) uses the damage splits of the host (`damageAttributed`, and `attributionRevision` once the host's window closes): the actor's damage, less what the buffs of other players and of non-players gave it, plus what its own buffs gave to the damage of other players, over the duration of `encDps`. Each skill has its own rDPS, and a buff's share of others' damage shows on the `status:<id>` skill of the player who gave it. rDPS is unavailable when the host does not send the splits, and for summaries saved before it. Each metric carries quality; unsupported shield is null. Direct hits alone define critical/direct-hit denominators. Recent DPS uses sixty source-second buckets and does not rewrite old buckets on post revisions or later pet-owner discovery. Separate/merged pet displays conserve totals; pet deaths remain separate.

The feature page `战斗统计` lists the newest 100 visits (zone entries) of the combat log that have a fight, newest first; the visit in progress is on top once its first fight starts. A visit is named by the duty its territory holds (the `Name` of its `ContentFinderCondition`, column 43, through column 10 of `TerritoryType`), otherwise by the territory's place name. Expanding a visit lists its fights with their result: a kill when the main enemy (the enemy that took the most damage from the parse scope) died in the fight, a wipe when every party member in scope died and did nothing after their last death, otherwise ended. Choosing a fight shows its damage table (DPS, rDPS, critical/direct-hit rates, damage taken, deaths, a total row) and a healing table for healers (HPS, overheal %); each actor row expands into its skills. A fight still held by this activation is shown from memory; any other is recomputed from the log of its visit by `src/log/replay.ts`, the same Encounters and Ledger the live statistics use, and the newest three recomputed visits stay in memory. A visit whose log cannot be read shows `日志不可用` and the reason. Viewing an earlier fight does not follow a new one; the page offers to return to it. Job and skill icons use `data.icon` when the host provides it and fall back to the job abbreviation and an empty icon slot otherwise. Settings and clearing history are on the plugin settings page (`settings.html`, placement `settings`). Summaries are no longer stored; the summaries of the old `history/` store are deleted on activation and not migrated.

The combat log keeps every statistics input in order, so a visit can be recomputed later and reviewed. It is written to `log/<local date>.parquet` in plugin storage, one file per day by the start of each visit (zone entry); a visit that crosses midnight stays in its first day's file. Near the single-file limit the day continues in `log/<date>-2.parquet` and so on; a visit may span files, a fight never does (a fight that alone exceeds the limit stops recording and its end note says `truncated`). The files are standard Parquet (`hyparquet`/`hyparquet-writer`, GZIP via `fflate`, no WebAssembly): one flat table, one row per record, with the first column `note` left uncompressed so the JSON notes for visit start/end and fight start/end (zone, members, times, end reason, result) can be read in a text editor. Each fight ends with one SHA-256 over all of its rows in its `fightEnd` note; reading a visit recomputes it and reports `日志校验失败` on a mismatch. Data is written every ten seconds while in a zone and after each fight and visit, so a crash loses at most ten seconds. The plugin asks the host for 2 GiB of storage and 16 MiB per file in `plugin.json`, about 500 hours of log at the rate below and about 3.9 hours for one fight. When the log would exceed the storage budget (`storage.pluginBytes` − 1 MiB − the game data cache), or would leave too few entries for the host to list the directory (`storage.listItems` − 16 files), the oldest whole file is deleted, never one holding the current visit.

Activation reads the log from the newest file back, until it holds the newest 100 visits with a fight that the page lists or 5 seconds have passed; today's files and the last file are always read, since writing continues in them. The older files stay in storage and the page does not show them. Their size is unknown without reading them, so they are only deleted when the host reports `quota_exceeded`, oldest first, and clearing history deletes them too. If reading the newest 100 visits takes longer than 5 seconds, the list shows fewer of them. Until the log has been evicted down, unread files can fill the storage kept for the game data cache, and a cache write then fails with a warning. Clearing history also clears the log except the current visit. `src/log/capacity.test.ts` measures one synthetic hour of eight-player fighting (10 action effects/s, three in ten hitting eight targets, one HP change per effect, periodic ticks, five status changes/s, four position samples/s for ten actors): 4,159,333 bytes in 486,626 rows. About 1.7 MiB of that is null levels, which data page v2 stores uncompressed. Set `MOJITO_CAPACITY_OUTPUT` to write the measurement.

Whether enemies can be targeted and when cutscenes play are logged for the review: a `targetable` row (`actor`, `flag`) for each `targetableChanged` of an enemy, and a `cutscene` row (`flag`) for each `cutsceneChanged`. They have their own subscription, so a host without these events only leaves them out with a warning. They do not reach the statistics of a replay. The review takes the last state of each before the fight as the one it starts in.

The potency samples of the host (`potencySample`) are logged for the curves of the review. They have their own subscription: a host without the periodic model rejects it, the plugin logs a warning and the statistics go on without them. Only the samples of members are kept. Each sample is a `potency` row followed by one `factor` row for each multiplier the host knew. To keep old files readable, these rows reuse existing columns, and some columns mean something else here:

| Row | Column | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `potency` | `actor`, `target`, `action`, `seq`, `amount` | caster, target, action, sequence of the action effect, damage |
| `potency` | `retained` | potency at the level of the caster, times the potency modifiers of the gauge |
| `potency` | `unknown` | product of the multipliers the host knew |
| `factor` | `status`, `actor` | status (0 for a modifier of the gauge) and its source |
| `factor` | `amount` | multiplier |
| `factor` | `flag` | the target holds the status (otherwise the caster does) |

A `factor` row without its `potency` row fails the log check. These rows do not reach the statistics of a replay.

The position map of the review zooms to where the actors are at the current time. It then holds still until a member no longer fits in a range 40 meters wide, and moves gradually to the next range. When members are far apart, more than 60 meters from anyone else, the map splits into equal panes, one for each place, each zoomed on its own. An enemy shows in the pane of the nearest member within 60 meters, and does not show otherwise. A map shows the same thing at a given time whether it was reached by playing or by dragging the timeline.

While the skill timeline is expanded, the timeline of the review has two more lanes under the ruler, which share its zoom, its playhead and its span selection:

- Damage per second: one line for each chosen member, the damage of each second averaged with the two seconds on each side. With pets merged, the damage of a pet counts for its owner. A member keeps its color, which follows its place in the filter bar.
- Damage per potency: for the member picked in the lane label, every sample as a dot (damage ÷ potency) and the window value as a line, the median of the samples within five seconds on each side; a sample with fewer than three samples in its window has no window value. The dashed line is the baseline: the median of the samples without known multipliers (of all samples when fewer than five have none), taken again without the samples whose window value is more than 5% away from that first median. Samples whose window value is more than 5% above or below the baseline make blue or red regions, which split where the direction changes or the samples pause for more than fifteen seconds. Moving the pointer onto a region opens a card with the reason: raid buffs, own buffs, statuses on the target and damage down, from the multipliers of the host and the Damage Down statuses on the member. When those do not explain the region, the card lists the statuses on the member and on the boss.

The damage splits of the host are logged too, so a replayed visit gives the same rDPS: each `damageAttributed` is an `attributed` row (the split, with `attacker` and `retained` in their own columns) followed by one `part` row for each buff that had a share in it, and each `attributionRevision` is a header row followed by one `delta` row for each changed split, each with its own `part` rows. The deltas of a host window that only closes with the zone change come after `zoneChanged` and are logged in the next visit, as periodic revisions are, so a replayed earlier visit keeps the realtime split for that fight. Log files written before the `attacker` and `retained` columns existed are skipped as `日志列与当前版本不一致`. The capacity test also measures the same hour with a split for every outgoing hit and tick, a quarter of them with two buff parts, and the deltas of those at each fight end: 5,577,217 bytes in 566,465 rows, against 4,166,203 bytes without them on the same Windows machine (written as `<MOJITO_CAPACITY_OUTPUT>.splits.json`).

## Validation and remaining acceptance

Synthetic unit tests cover strict periodic expiry/source clocks, ordered projections, effect/AoE counts, same-name identity, pet grouping, conservative revisions, recent buckets, source/manual/quiet boundaries, quota/failure/clear/eviction behavior and real UTF-8 capacity. `src/log/replay.test.ts` replays logged visits and compares them with the live summaries the page shows, and recomputes the visits of an earlier activation through the page commands.

After building the launcher/runtime and plugin, run `scripts/plugin-isolation/combat-statistics-main.cjs` with the production Electron binary and an absolute disposable `MOJITO_STATISTICS_OUTPUT`. It uses synthetic events through the real host and Worker loader, opens the actual feature, and checks two encounters, the quiet end, choosing an earlier fight and returning to the current one, a fight recomputed from the log after restart, and clearing history from the settings page. It also checks the actual statistics Worker output in all the original templates and saves their rendered evidence. This default mode also needs `pnpm --filter @ffcafe/mojito-overlay-plugins build`: the script installs those plugins into its disposable profile and grants them access to the statistics service. `MOJITO_STATISTICS_CLEANUP_ONLY=1` only checks that a normal stop flushes the log and does not need them.

Overheal was replayed from two real recordings (an extreme trial and two savage-tier arenas, 8-player parties) against their ACT network logs: no healing stayed unmeasured, and overheal was 60.9% and 81.1% of healing. EffectResult arrived about 1.1 seconds after a heal (2 seconds at most); about a quarter of direct heals in the first recording and half in the second had none. Healed HoT ticks never shared a timestamp with an UpdateHpMpTp of their target, and ticks on full-HP targets were fully overhealed. Windows execution, comparison to ACT, real long-recording memory/latency measurement and all-template compatibility acceptance remain required. Synthetic production-loader success is not real-game or Windows acceptance.
